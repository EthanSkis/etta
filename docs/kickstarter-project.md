## ⚠️ Read this before you build the campaign

**Kickstarter's rules prohibit projects "centered around establishing and
providing services (travel agent services, life coaching, food delivery,
etc.)."** Etta is a subscription phone-call service. As written, this campaign
is likely to be rejected in Kickstarter's project review, and the reward tiers
below that sell months of Etta are the specific thing the rule names.

That is not a reason to skip the work — everything below is the campaign, and
most of it (story, video script, funding breakdown, risks) transfers to any
platform. But pick the platform deliberately:

| Platform | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Kickstarter** | ❌ Poor | Services prohibited; rewards can't be subscriptions. Reviewable only if reframed around a physical/creative artifact (see §9). |
| **Indiegogo** | ✅ Good | Permits service and software campaigns; flexible funding; InDemand keeps the page up after the campaign. |
| **Wefunder / Republic** | ✅✅ Best for "get funded" | Reg CF equity crowdfunding. You're raising for a startup with a real B2B2C thesis — this is the instrument that actually matches. Backers become investors, not customers you owe 6 months of calls to. |

If the literal goal is *money into Etta*, Reg CF is the honest answer.
If the goal is *proof that families want this*, Indiegogo with the story below
does that in 30 days. The draft is written so it works on all three.

Also note the site's own footer currently says **"Etta is an early-stage
concept. This page is a design draft — not a live service."** That has to come
down or be reconciled before any campaign points at ettacalls.com.

## 3. Story

### The gap between "alive" and "okay"

Millions of us live too far from our parents to check on them daily. We carry a
low-grade worry that never resolves: *Did she eat today? Did she fall? Is she
lonelier than she lets on? Is she declining in some slow way I can't see over
the phone once a fortnight?*

Every existing answer fails somewhere. Calling every day yourself is
unsustainable — you will miss days, and the missing is its own guilt. Volunteer
telephone-reassurance programs are free and kind and have eight-to-ten-week
waitlists. Human call services run $30–220 a month. Sensors and wearables
($25–70/month plus hardware) tell you that movement happened; they tell you
nothing about mood, meals, or state of mind. Caregiver apps organise the
family and never once touch the senior.

So there's a gap. On one side: *my parent is technically alive.* On the other:
*I actually know how my parent is doing.* Nothing sits in the middle.

### What Etta does

Etta calls. An ordinary phone call, to an ordinary phone, at a time your parent
picked. Nothing to download, nothing to charge, nothing to learn, nothing new
on the kitchen counter.

The call opens exactly like this:

> "Hi Margaret, it's Etta — I'm an AI assistant, calling to check in like your
> daughter Sarah set up."

Then it's a conversation. How they slept. Whether they've eaten. Whether the
tablets got taken. What they're up to today. And then whatever they actually
want to talk about, for as long as they want to talk about it.

Afterwards you get a text — from Etta's own number, not an app, not an inbox.
Overall mood. Anything notable: *mentioned her knee is worse. Sounded brighter
than yesterday.* Any flags. If nobody picks up, Etta retries, texts your
parent, then works down the escalation chain you set.

Over weeks those become trends — sleep, mood, appetite, engagement, plotted
over time. That's the part that matters. No single phone call reveals slow
decline. Fourteen of them in a row do.

### The part everyone else gets wrong

The person who pays and the person who gets called are two different people.
Almost every product in this category treats that as an inconvenience. We treat
it as the whole design.

**Your parent consents, personally, on a recorded call, before Etta ever dials
them.** Not a checkbox you tick on their behalf. Not a line in a terms page.
Their own voice, their own phone, usually with you sitting right there. Until
that happens, nothing is charged and no call is placed.

**Etta always says she's an AI.** Every call, in the first sentence, in plain
words. No warm ambiguity, no "virtual companion" hedging.

**"Stop calling me" works the first time.** Said to Etta, on any call, in any
phrasing — the calls end immediately and so does the billing. It's enforced in
the code, not in a support queue.

**No sales content ever appears inside a call.** Not an upsell, not a referral,
not a partner mention. Ever.

We're doing this because the category is one scandal away from collapse, and
because it's simply the right way to build something that talks to somebody's
mother. A journalist who goes looking for the ugly version of AI eldercare
should find that Etta is the counterexample.

### What your money unblocks

Two things stand between this and real families, and neither is code.

**A lawyer.** The consent mechanics when the buyer and the called party differ
is a genuine TCPA question, and the FCC has an AI-disclosure rule pending. We
will not dial a stranger's mother on a guess. One telecom attorney, properly
paid, is the first line in the budget.

**Carrier registration.** Every summary text we've sent has come back blocked —
error 30034, unregistered A2P sender. US carriers don't let an application text
you from a normal number until the brand and campaign are registered. It costs
money and takes weeks. It's line two.

Then: six months of calls for 300 pilot families, and the care-circle features
that turn per-call summaries into something a family can actually act on.

### Where this goes

Near term, Etta is the daily voice in the gap between visits.

Long term, the institutions that pay for eldercare — Medicare Advantage plans,
employers with eldercare benefits, senior-living operators — save real money
when decline is caught early and loneliness is caught at all. That's the
business. Consumer-phase retention and satisfaction data is the evidence for
that pitch, which is why we're collecting it from day one, and why 300 real
pilot families matters more than any amount of deck.

## 5. Risks and challenges

*Kickstarter requires this field. Do not soften it — the honest version is
persuasive on its own.*

**The legal question is real and it is not resolved.** Getting consent when the
person paying and the person being called are different people is a genuine
TCPA question, and the FCC has an AI-disclosure rule pending that could change
the requirements mid-flight. This is the first line in our budget and a hard
gate on launch. If counsel tells us the flow needs rebuilding, we rebuild it
before we dial, and backers get told immediately. We will not ship around a
legal answer we don't like.

**Carrier registration can take longer than anyone plans for.** A2P 10DLC brand
and campaign vetting is opaque and occasionally slow. Voice calls are
unaffected and work today; it's the summary texts that are gated. If
registration drags, families get summaries through the web view — which already
works, with no login — while we wait.

**The unit economics are tight and we know exactly how tight.** Voice AI runs
roughly $0.05–0.15/minute today. Daily long calls at consumer prices are
margin-negative, which is why calls are designed at 3–5 minutes and why
frequency is tiered. Model costs are falling, but we're not betting the company
on that curve — the pricing works at today's costs.

**This feature is easy to copy.** Six competitors already exist. Consumer prices
are being competed toward $10–30. Our answer isn't the calling — it's the
coordination layer for the family and, eventually, the payer channel that none
of them has touched. If we're wrong about that, we're a commodity.

**The category could lose public trust overnight.** One undisclosed-AI scandal
involving somebody's grandmother sets the whole category back years. Our
protection is that we'd survive the story: disclosure in the first sentence,
recorded consent, instant revocation, no sales content. That's not marketing
copy — it's enforced in the code, and it's the reason to back us specifically.

**What qualifies us:** the pipeline is built and a real call has already run end
to end — scheduler, consent gate, live call, structured summary, escalation
logic, billing that refuses to charge until the senior agrees. The remaining
work is a lawyer, a carrier registration, and 300 families. Not a prototype.

## 7. Campaign FAQ

**Does my parent know it's an AI?**
Yes, in the first sentence of every single call, in plain words. If that's a
dealbreaker for your family, Etta is the wrong product, and we'd rather you
know now.

**What if they don't want it?**
Then it doesn't happen. Your parent gives their own recorded yes before any
call is placed, and you are never charged until they do. If they decline, the
trial cancels itself.

**Can they stop it?**
Any call, any wording. "Stop calling me" ends the service on the spot — calls
and billing both. It's in the code, not the support queue.

**Do they need a smartphone / app / wifi?**
No. Any phone that rings. That's the entire requirement.

**Do I need an app?**
No. Summaries arrive as texts. The family view is a link, no login, no account.

**Is this a medical or emergency service?**
No, and we make no medical or dementia-detection claims. Etta is not for
emergencies — call 911. The conversational-decline research is a real track for
us, but it's research, not a marketing line, and it won't become one until
there's evidence.

**What happens to the recordings?**
Your parent decides whether family can hear the calls — not you, not us. Data
is minimised by design; see ettacalls.com/privacy.

**What if you don't hit the goal?**
Nobody is charged and we keep building slower. We'd rather return the money than
launch under-resourced at something this sensitive.

## 9. If you insist on Kickstarter

The rule bans projects centered on *providing a service*. It does not ban
projects centered on *making a thing*. The only honest reframe is to make the
thing real and primary:

> **"What My Mother Said"** — a printed, large-print keepsake book. A year of a
> parent's daily calls, distilled into the things they actually said: the
> stories, the recurring jokes, the neighbour's dog. One copy for the family,
> one for the senior. The calls are how it gets made; the book is what backers
> receive.

That is a genuine creative artifact, it is a genuine deliverable, and it is
genuinely the most moving thing this product could produce. It would also pass
review.

But be clear-eyed: it funds a book, not a company. The $35,000 above is for
lawyers and carrier registration and six months of calls. Backers of a book
have not agreed to fund that. If the money you need is company money, go to
Wefunder or Republic and raise it as what it is.
